F1 2026 Lap-Time Simulation & Race Engineering

AVL VSM simulation-led optimisation of a 2026-regulation Formula 1 vehicle model at the Red Bull Ring. The study treated lap time as a systems problem: aerodynamic drag/downforce trade-off, active aero balance, ride-height sensitivity, tyre and load-transfer behaviour, MGU-K deployment, gearbox usage and fuel constraints were evaluated together.

Project summary

What this project demonstrates

This case study demonstrates a systems-level race engineering workflow: define a 2026-regulation baseline, run virtual tests, optimise aerodynamic and mechanical setup parameters, tune powertrain deployment, and evaluate the final result using lap time and vehicle behaviour rather than isolated subsystem metrics.

Engineering question

How can a 2026-regulation F1 vehicle model be optimised at the Red Bull Ring while balancing aero efficiency, handling stability, ERS deployment, gearbox behaviour and fuel constraints?

01 — Track and workflow

Race Engineering Context

The Red Bull Ring was used as the target circuit because it forces a setup compromise between straight-line efficiency, heavy braking stability and mixed-speed cornering. Long acceleration zones reward low drag and strong ERS deployment, while the corner sequence still requires sufficient downforce, tyre support and aero balance stability.

02 — Aerodynamic optimisation

Aerodynamic Setup, Active Aero & Ride Height

Aerodynamic optimisation focused on recovering performance inside the reduced 2026 aerodynamic envelope. The key challenge was improving speed and efficiency without creating excessive balance migration during braking, deployment zones or corner transitions.

Aero slide 01

2024 vs 2026 aerodynamic baseline

This plot establishes the regulation-driven performance gap between the 2024 reference and the 2026 baseline. The 2026 model produces reduced downforce and drag, giving higher efficiency but less cornering load. The key issue is how the reduced aerodynamic envelope affects balance migration under braking, cornering and deployment zones.

Aero slide 02

Active aero deployment

Front and rear wing offsets were applied over lap distance to model active aero behaviour. In AVL VSM, these wing-angle inputs shift the base aero map rather than representing literal physical wing angles, so the setup must be interpreted as map-offset control rather than direct geometry change.

Aero slide 03

Drag reduction versus balance stability

This comparison shows why the lowest-drag configuration is not automatically the best race setup. The stronger drag-reduction option can generate larger aero-balance movement, while the more stable option sacrifices some efficiency for rear stability and driver confidence.

Aero slide 04

Ride-height lap-time sensitivity

The ride-height map shows the aero platform sensitivity of the 2026 model. Low front ride height with moderate rear ride height produced the strongest lap-time region, indicating that front ground-clearance control was especially important for load generation and balance.

Aero slide 05

Optimised versus baseline aero result

The optimised setup improved straight-line speed in deployment zones through targeted drag reduction while keeping off-deployment drag, downforce and aero balance close to the target operating window. The aero gain is useful because it improves lap time without destabilising the car outside activation zones.

03 — Vehicle dynamics optimisation

Tyre Behaviour, Setup Sensitivity & Mechanical Balance

Vehicle dynamics optimisation was used to identify which mechanical setup parameters had the largest lap-time authority. The goal was to separate real performance drivers from low-value tuning changes.

VD slide 01

Vehicle dynamics workflow

The workflow starts from tyre behaviour and skid-pad response before moving into full-circuit setup changes. This matters because lap-time changes need to be traced back to physical mechanisms: tyre force generation, balance, load transfer, ride response and pitch sensitivity.

VD slide 02

Tyre force behaviour

The longitudinal tyre-force plot shows how force generation varies with slip ratio and vertical load. Higher vertical load increases available longitudinal force, but the relationship is not perfectly linear, which is critical for braking, traction and load-transfer analysis.

VD slide 03

Setup sensitivity ranking

The sensitivity chart ranks setup changes by lap-time effect relative to the 2026 baseline. Alignment and camber changes show especially strong influence, highlighting that mechanical setup still has major lap-time authority even in an aero-led vehicle model.

04 — Powertrain and race strategy

ERS Deployment, Gearbox Usage & Fuel Constraint

Powertrain optimisation focused on using the 2026 MGU-K and energy limits effectively. The setup needed to support acceleration while respecting deployment, traction and fuel-use constraints.

Powertrain slide 01

MGU-K / ERS deployment

Electrical power delivery was shaped over lap distance rather than deployed blindly. Sector-based deployment helps align torque delivery with traction availability, straights and energy limits, reducing wheelspin risk while supporting acceleration where it matters most.

Powertrain slide 02

Gearbox optimisation

The gearbox position plot shows whether the car is using the available ratios effectively across the lap. A poor ratio set can trap the vehicle in unsuitable gears, limiting acceleration and forcing inefficient braking or shift behaviour. The optimised setup improves gear utilisation across key zones.

Powertrain slide 03

Fuel consumption strategy

Under 2026-style constraints, fuel use is linked to energy flow, so throttle mapping and high-RPM behaviour affect both performance and legality. Comparing full-throttle and limited-throttle behaviour shows how race strategy can manage fuel without treating energy use and lap time separately.

05 — Integrated result

Final Lap-Time Result

The final result shows the value of system integration. The optimised 2026 setup reduced simulated lap time from 72.075 s to 66.350 s, a 5.725 s improvement over the baseline. The optimised car remained slower than the 2024 reference, which is expected because the 2026 model is constrained by lower aerodynamic load and updated powertrain limits.

Key result

The simulated F1 2026 lap time improved from 72.075 s to 66.350 s, giving a 5.725 s improvement over the baseline.

Engineering takeaway

The gain came from coordinated subsystem tuning rather than a single isolated change: aero deployment, ride height, mechanical setup, ERS strategy, gearbox usage and fuel constraints all contributed to the final performance envelope.

06 — Reflection

Limitations & Next Steps

Limitations

  • Group project, so subsystem ownership belongs within a collaborative workflow.
  • 2026 aerodynamic model used scaled aero maps rather than CFD or wind-tunnel-derived data.
  • Powertrain maps were regulation-informed rather than manufacturer-specific engine data.
  • Simulation result is virtual and not track-validated.

Future work

  • Replace scaled aero maps with CFD-derived 2026 aerodynamic data.
  • Validate powertrain maps against higher-fidelity PU data.
  • Include tyre degradation and stint-based race optimisation.
  • Run robustness studies across fuel load, track temperature and driver model variation.
