Composite Chassis Design & Impact Modelling

Composite chassis and crash-energy management case study combining laminate strategy, impact attenuator concept comparison and LS-DYNA explicit impact simulation. The work focused on balancing energy absorption, chassis deformation control and material/geometry selection under severe frontal and side-impact loading.

Project summary

What this project demonstrates

This project demonstrates a structural CAE workflow for composite crashworthiness: define the chassis protection problem, model impact loading, compare material/geometry concepts, and make an engineering recommendation based on energy absorption and residual chassis deformation rather than a single metric.

Engineering question

Which composite or hybrid attenuator concept gives the best compromise between crash-energy absorption and chassis protection under severe impact loading?

Objectives

  • Chassis protection: reduce frontal impact deformation relative to the bare chassis baseline.
  • Material strategy: compare aluminium, honeycomb, foam and carbon-fibre-based concepts.
  • Crash modelling: build an explicit LS-DYNA workflow with realistic contact, boundary conditions and impact energy.
  • Decision logic: select the best concept using both absorbed energy and chassis displacement.
01 — Chassis design context

Composite Chassis & Packaging Strategy

The design problem started from a composite chassis/enclosure architecture where impact structures, internal packaging and laminate strategy had to be considered together.

Chassis architecture

Packaging-led composite structure

The chassis design phase defined the structural envelope and packaging constraints before moving into crashworthiness assessment. This helped frame the attenuator as part of a larger composite system, rather than an isolated block added after the chassis was designed.

  • Composite chassis/enclosure concept with impact protection zones.
  • Internal packaging constraints considered alongside structural design.
  • Design intent focused on controlled crush behaviour and chassis protection.
02 — Simulation setup

LS-DYNA Impact Simulation Setup

The impact model used explicit transient dynamics to simulate high-strain-rate crash loading. The setup included a 1300 kg impactor, initial velocity of 13.86 m/s, fixed chassis boundary conditions and contact definitions between the impactor, attenuator and chassis.

Material models

  • MAT 054 for enhanced composite damage modelling.
  • MAT 058 for woven laminate representation where appropriate.
  • MAT 126 for aluminium honeycomb crush behaviour.

Modelling approach

  • Layered shell elements for composite laminates.
  • Solid elements for aluminium honeycomb core regions.
  • Automatic crash contacts and tied connections for bonded interfaces.
03 — Concept comparison

Impact Attenuator Design Concepts

Five attenuator concepts were compared to understand how material selection and internal geometry affected crush behaviour, energy absorption and load transfer into the chassis.

04 — Crashworthiness results

Energy Absorption & Chassis Deformation

The bare chassis baseline produced 64.9 mm frontal displacement, making frontal impact the critical case. The final design decision therefore focused on reducing chassis deformation while still absorbing a high proportion of the impact energy.

Selected concept

Design 5: T800 tube-block hybrid

Design 5 used a hybrid tube-block geometry with T800 UD material to promote controlled crushing. It absorbed 81.9 kJ while reducing chassis displacement to approximately 14 mm, making it the best compromise between energy absorption and chassis protection.

  • More deformation-controlled than the higher-stiffness interlocking T1100 concept.
  • Lower transmitted residual structural load than the maximum-energy design.
  • Best overall compromise for chassis protection within the tested concepts.
05 — Engineering decision

Design Recommendation

Why not simply choose Design 4?

Design 4 absorbed the highest energy at approximately 92 kJ, but its stiffer interlocking T1100 structure transmitted more residual load to the chassis, resulting in approximately 27 mm displacement.

Why Design 5 was selected

Design 5 absorbed slightly less energy at 81.9 kJ, but reduced chassis displacement to approximately 14 mm. That made it the stronger engineering compromise for chassis protection.

06 — Results

Headline Results

  • Baseline frontal displacement: 64.9 mm without an attenuator.
  • Selected concept: Design 5, T800 tube-block hybrid attenuator.
  • Energy absorbed: 81.9 kJ for Design 5.
  • Chassis displacement: approximately 14 mm for Design 5.
  • Engineering trade-off: Design 4 absorbed more energy, but Design 5 gave better deformation control.
07 — Reflection

Limitations & Next Steps

Limitations

  • Simulation-led study without physical crash-test validation.
  • Material behaviour depends heavily on assumed LS-DYNA material card inputs.
  • Mesh refinement was balanced against coursework simulation time constraints.
  • Design goal was approached, but the selected concept did not reach the original 5 mm target.

Future work

  • Perform finer mesh sensitivity studies around crush initiation zones.
  • Validate material cards against coupon or component-level test data.
  • Optimise lay-up sequence and ply orientation for progressive crushing.
  • Extend the selected frontal design into a side-impact-specific geometry.
