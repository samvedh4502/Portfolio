LM-GT3 Aerodynamic Development

Simulation-led aerodynamic development of an LMGT3-style concept, combining CAD surface design, CFD validation, aero-package iteration, aero-map generation and simulated lap-time evaluation. The project followed an end-to-end workflow from regulatory packaging and CFD-ready geometry through to aerodynamic performance metrics and vehicle-level impact.

Project summary

What this project demonstrates

This case study demonstrates a complete motorsport aero development workflow: surface design, simulation credibility, controlled aerodynamic iteration, aero-map generation and lap-time interpretation. The emphasis is not only on producing CFD plots, but on turning aerodynamic evidence into engineering decisions.

Engineering question

How can an LMGT3-style aerodynamic package be improved for efficiency, stability and robustness, while connecting the CFD results to vehicle-level performance rather than treating downforce and drag coefficients in isolation?

Objectives

  • Surface design: create CFD-ready aerodynamic surfaces within an LMGT3-style packaging envelope.
  • CFD credibility: establish a baseline through mesh convergence, near-wall checks and reference validation.
  • Controlled iteration: compare splitter, floor, diffuser, wide-body and rear-wing changes like-for-like.
  • Performance metrics: judge iterations using efficiency, load contribution, yaw robustness and balance sensitivity.
  • Vehicle impact: convert CFD outputs into aero maps and evaluate simulated lap-time effect at Portimão.
01 — Surface design

Surface Design & Regulatory Packaging

The project began with surface development around an LMGT3-style regulatory envelope. The goal was to create clean, CFD-ready aerodynamic geometry that allowed controlled comparison between iterations.

CAD-to-CFD foundation

CFD-ready aerodynamic surfaces

The surface design focused on aerodynamic intent and simulation usability: clean intersections, controlled edges, repeatable geometry changes and clear device zones for the splitter, underbody, diffuser and rear wing.

  • Front diffuser-style splitter and air-dam treatment.
  • Floor skirts and wide-body surfaces for underbody sealing and wheel-wake control.
  • High-chord NACA 6412 rear wing with gurney flap and lip spoiler.
  • Defined aerodynamic boundary zones for controlled iteration.
02 — CFD credibility

CFD Methodology & Validation

Before comparing aerodynamic iterations, the CFD setup was checked for mesh quality, convergence behaviour and near-wall resolution so the deltas were defensible.

01 — Regulation-informed geometry setup

The vehicle was divided into splitter, underbody, diffuser and rear-wing zones to support controlled aerodynamic development.

02 — Mesh refinement strategy

Local refinement was applied around the underbody, wheel wake, diffuser and rear wake to capture key flow structures.

03 — Drag coefficient convergence

The selected mesh stabilised around 14M cells and was checked against published DrivAer reference data.

04 — Near-wall resolution check

The wall-resolved setup targeted y+ < 5 across major surfaces to support low-y+ turbulence modelling.

03 — Aerodynamic development

Aerodynamic Development & Flow Physics

The aerodynamic package was developed through controlled design iterations. The focus was not only increasing downforce, but understanding how each device changed the flow field, load generation and robustness.

Flow-field interpretation

Why the final package worked

The final iteration combined a Ferrari 499P-inspired splitter, floor skirts, wide-body treatment, air dam, shark fin and high-chord rear wing. Pressure coefficient and wall shear stress plots were used to interpret the effect of these surfaces on suction generation, underbody sealing and wake control.

  • Stronger front suction from the splitter and air-dam package.
  • Improved underbody sealing through skirts and wide-body treatment.
  • More coherent diffuser and rear-wing flow structures.
  • Reduced sensitivity to yaw compared with weaker configurations.
04 — Aero metrics

Aero Performance Metrics & Robustness

Each iteration was assessed using drag/downforce trade-off, lift-to-drag ratio and yaw robustness. This avoided selecting a package based only on peak downforce.

05 — Vehicle simulation

Aero Mapping & Vehicle Simulation

CFD force coefficients were converted into aero maps using MATLAB interpolation. This allowed the package to be evaluated at vehicle level through speed trace and simulated lap-time analysis rather than isolated CFD outputs.

01 — Downforce aero map

CLA values were interpolated across front and rear ride-height conditions to create a continuous downforce surface for simulator input.

02 — Drag aero map

CDA was mapped across the same operating space, allowing drag penalties and efficiency trade-offs to be considered alongside downforce.

03 — Speed trace comparison

The speed-distance trace shows where aerodynamic changes translated into vehicle-level gains around the Portimão lap.

04 — Simulated lap-time impact

Iteration 6 produced the fastest simulated lap, reducing lap time from 102.18s to 98.21s compared with the baseline.

06 — Results

Headline Results

  • Predicted L/D = 2.24 for the final aerodynamic package.
  • +23.6% downforce improvement relative to mid-iteration comparison.
  • -7.7% drag reduction relative to mid-iteration comparison.
  • 78% downforce retention at 5° yaw, indicating improved crosswind robustness.
  • 102.18s → 98.21s simulated Portimão lap time, a 3.97s simulated reduction.
07 — Engineering reflection

Limitations & Next Steps

The project is strongest as a relative aerodynamic development study. The results are useful for comparing design directions, but should not be presented as physically validated full-scale race-car performance.

Limitations

  • Scaled DrivAer-based geometry rather than a full production LMGT3 scan.
  • Steady RANS used for straight-line studies, with URANS used for yaw assessment.
  • No physical wind-tunnel or track validation of the final package.
  • Lap-time results are simulated and dependent on model assumptions.

Future work

  • Wind-tunnel validation using pressure taps and force-balance data.
  • Higher-fidelity transient CFD such as DES/LES for wake and diffuser behaviour.
  • Coupled suspension and tyre model integration for dynamic ride-height sensitivity.
  • Expanded aero maps across larger yaw, pitch and ride-height operating windows.
