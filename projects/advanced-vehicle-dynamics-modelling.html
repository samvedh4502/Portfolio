Advanced Vehicle Dynamics: Handling & Suspension Modelling

ADAMS-based vehicle dynamics study covering linear bicycle modelling, twin-track handling analysis, lateral weight transfer, yaw-rate response and suspension ride optimisation. The project connects first-principles vehicle dynamics equations with simulation outputs to assess handling balance, tyre loading, ride response and pitch behaviour.

Project summary

What this project demonstrates

This project demonstrates the progression from simplified handling models to more detailed vehicle dynamics representations. A linear bicycle model was used to understand fundamental yaw and lateral-force response, while a twin-track model was used to capture individual wheel loading and weight transfer. Suspension modelling then progressed from SDOF and TDOF systems to a 4DOF pitch model.

Engineering question

How can simplified and intermediate-fidelity ADAMS models be used to evaluate vehicle handling balance, tyre loading, ride response and suspension parameter sensitivity?

Model basis

Core Equations Used in the Vehicle Dynamics Models

The ADAMS models were supported by first-principles vehicle dynamics equations covering tyre slip, lateral force generation, aerodynamic drag, yaw response, weight transfer and suspension motion. These equations define the physical basis behind the simulation outputs without turning the page into a full derivation.

Slip angle estimation

Slip angle was used as the fundamental tyre-state variable for lateral force generation.

\[ \alpha = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{V_y}{V_x}\right) \]

Linear lateral force

For small slip angles, the tyre was approximated using a linear cornering stiffness model.

\[ F_y = C_\alpha \alpha \]

Nonlinear tyre force form

A Magic Formula-style expression was identified as the next step for nonlinear tyre behaviour.

\[ F_y = D \sin \left[ C \tan^{-1} \left( B\alpha - E(B\alpha - \tan^{-1}(B\alpha)) \right) \right] \]

Aerodynamic drag force

Drag was included as a longitudinal resistance term affecting vehicle speed and handling response.

\[ F_{drag} = \frac{1}{2}\rho C_d A V_x^2 \]

Driving force approximation

A simplified drive-force relationship was used to overcome longitudinal resistance.

\[ F_{driving} = 0.25 \left(V_{input}^{2}\right) \]

Low-speed yaw-rate approximation

At low speed, yaw response was estimated from forward speed, steering input and wheelbase.

\[ \dot{\psi} = \frac{V_x \delta}{L} \]

Static axle normal loads

Static load distribution was calculated from vehicle mass, wheelbase and CG position.

\[ F_{z,front} = \frac{mgb}{L} \] \[ F_{z,rear} = \frac{mga}{L} \]

Longitudinal weight transfer

Longitudinal acceleration transfers load between front and rear axles.

\[ \Delta F_{z,long} = \frac{m a_x h_{cg}}{L} \]

Lateral weight transfer

Cornering acceleration transfers load between inside and outside wheels.

\[ \Delta F_{z,lat} = \frac{m a_y h_{cg}}{T} \]

Individual wheel loads

For the twin-track model, axle loads were resolved into individual wheel normal forces.

\[ F_{z,FL} = F_{z,front} + F_{z,front,lat} - F_{z,long} \] \[ F_{z,FR} = F_{z,front} - F_{z,front,lat} - F_{z,long} \] \[ F_{z,RL} = F_{z,rear} + F_{z,rear,lat} + F_{z,long} \] \[ F_{z,RR} = F_{z,rear} - F_{z,rear,lat} + F_{z,long} \]

SDOF suspension response

The single-degree-of-freedom suspension model was treated as a mass-spring-damper system.

\[ m\ddot{y} + c\dot{y} + ky = F_{ext} \]

Natural frequency

Natural frequency defines the system’s tendency to oscillate without damping.

\[ \omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} \]

Damping ratio

Damping ratio defines whether the suspension response is underdamped, critically damped or overdamped.

\[ \zeta = \frac{c}{2\sqrt{km}} \]

TDOF sprung-mass equation

The TDOF model introduced sprung and unsprung mass interaction through the suspension spring and damper.

\[ m_s\ddot{y}_s(t) + c_s\left(\dot{y}_s(t)-\dot{y}_u(t)\right) + k_s\left(y_s(t)-y_u(t)\right) = 0 \]

TDOF unsprung-mass equation

Road excitation was applied through the unsprung mass side of the suspension model.

\[ m_u\ddot{y}_u(t) + c_u\dot{y}_u(t) + k_u y_u(t) = F_{ext}(t) \]

Relative displacement

Relative motion between sprung and unsprung masses drives spring and damper force.

\[ \Delta y(t) = y_s(t) - y_u(t) \]

Spring force

The spring force is proportional to relative suspension displacement.

\[ F_s = k_s \Delta y(t) \]

Damping force

The damping force is proportional to relative velocity across the suspension.

\[ F_d = c_s\left(\dot{y}_s(t)-\dot{y}_u(t)\right) \]

4DOF body vertical motion

The 4DOF model extended the ride model to include front and rear axle excitation effects.

\[ m_s\ddot{X}_s + c_{sf}(\dot{X}_{sf}-\dot{X}_{uf}) + k_{sf}(X_{sf}-X_{uf}) + c_{sr}(\dot{X}_{sr}-\dot{X}_{ur}) - f_{sf} - f_{sr} = 0 \]

4DOF pitch moment

Pitch response was driven by unequal front and rear suspension forces acting through their moment arms.

\[ I_s\ddot{\theta}_s + L_f \left[ c_{sf}(\dot{X}_{sf}-\dot{X}_{uf}) + k_{sf}(X_{sf}-X_{uf}) - f_{sf} \right] - L_r \left[ c_{sr}(\dot{X}_{sr}-\dot{X}_{ur}) - f_{sr} \right] = 0 \]

Front unsprung vertical motion

The front unsprung mass equation captures wheel-side vertical response under road excitation.

\[ m_{uf}\ddot{X}_{uf} - c_{sf}(\dot{X}_{sf}-\dot{X}_{uf}) - k_{sf}(X_{sf}-X_{uf}) + k_{uf}(X_{uf}-X_{sf}) + f_{sf} = 0 \]

Rear unsprung vertical motion

The rear unsprung mass equation captures rear wheel vertical response and its contribution to pitch.

\[ m_{ur}\ddot{X}_{ur} - c_{sr}(\dot{X}_{sr}-\dot{X}_{ur}) - k_{sr}(X_{sr}-X_{ur}) + k_{ur}(X_{ur}-X_{sr}) + f_{sr} = 0 \]
01 — Model setup

Bicycle Model Foundation

The bicycle model simplified the vehicle into front and rear tyre representations to study lateral and yaw behaviour. This made it possible to isolate understeer, neutral steer and oversteer response before moving to a higher-fidelity twin-track model.

02 — Handling analysis

Bicycle and Twin-Track Handling Response

The handling analysis compared yaw-rate and lateral-force behaviour across understeer, neutral steer and oversteer cases. The twin-track model added individual wheel loads, allowing lateral weight transfer and asymmetric tyre loading to be represented more realistically than the bicycle model.

Handling slide 01

Yaw-rate response across handling states

The yaw-rate comparison separates the three handling behaviours. Understeer produces a weaker rotational response and reduced turning authority, neutral steer gives a balanced response, while oversteer shows a sharper yaw response and higher sensitivity to steering input.

Handling slide 02

Twin-track model development

The twin-track model extends the bicycle model by representing all four tyres individually. This allows the simulation to capture left-right load transfer, axle interaction and asymmetric tyre force generation, which are not visible in a single-track representation.

Handling slide 03

Cornering weight transfer

During acceleration and cornering, vertical load transfers from front to rear and from the inside tyres to the outside tyres. In the oversteer case, the outer rear tyre carries the highest load while the inner front becomes the least loaded, directly influencing lateral force capacity and stability.

Handling slide 04

Bicycle versus twin-track comparison

The bicycle and twin-track models follow similar overall trends, but the twin-track model gives a more realistic response because it includes individual wheel loading and axle interaction. This is especially important when assessing oversteer and lateral force distribution.

03 — Suspension optimisation

Ride Response, Damper Tuning & Pitch Behaviour

The suspension section progressed from SDOF theory to a TDOF sprung/unsprung mass model and then to a 4DOF pitch representation. The TDOF model was optimised using RMS contact patch force variation, linking ride tuning to tyre-road contact consistency.

Suspension slide 01

TDOF spring and damper optimisation

The TDOF suspension model was optimised using RMS contact patch force variation as the objective. The selected setup reduced the damper coefficient from 6.3498 Ns/mm to 4.4694 Ns/mm and spring stiffness from 100 N/mm to 25 N/mm, reducing oscillatory response and improving tyre contact behaviour.

Suspension slide 02

Damper spline and frequency response

The damper spline and Bode gain comparison show how damping characteristics affect vibration amplification across frequency. The optimised response reduces resonance peaks and provides a more controlled gain profile, improving ride comfort and contact patch stability.

Suspension slide 03

4DOF pitch response

The 4DOF model introduced body pitch by applying asymmetric road inputs to the front and rear axles. This demonstrates how delayed axle excitation can create pitch motion, making the model more suitable for evaluating ride behaviour beyond simple vertical bounce.

04 — Results

Engineering Outcomes

Handling behaviour

The bicycle model gave a clean first-order understanding of understeer, neutral steer and oversteer. The twin-track model improved realism by resolving load transfer and individual wheel forces.

Weight transfer

The twin-track model showed the redistribution of normal load during acceleration and cornering, with the outer wheels becoming more heavily loaded and the inner front becoming least loaded during the oversteer case.

Suspension optimisation

The TDOF optimisation reduced RMS contact patch force variation by selecting lower spring stiffness and revised damping, improving vibration control and ride behaviour.

Pitch modelling

The 4DOF model introduced pitch response under asymmetric road input, extending the analysis from simple ride displacement to front-rear vehicle body motion.

05 — Reflection

Limitations & Next Steps

Limitations

  • Tyre force behaviour was simplified and did not fully use a nonlinear tyre model.
  • The bicycle model is useful for trends but cannot capture individual wheel load transfer.
  • The 4DOF model focused on pitch rather than a complete full-vehicle ride model.
  • Results are simulation-based and not validated against physical rig or track data.

Future work

  • Introduce nonlinear tyre models such as Pacejka / Magic Formula.
  • Add Ackermann steering geometry and more realistic steering kinematics.
  • Extend the 4DOF model into a full vehicle ride and handling model.
  • Explore semi-active damping and active suspension control strategies.
