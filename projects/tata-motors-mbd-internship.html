Project summary

What this experience demonstrates

This internship demonstrates applied vehicle dynamics simulation in an automotive OEM environment. The project focused on how suspension kinematics, compliance characteristics and steering response influence the steady-state handling behaviour of a compact SUV.

Kinematics and Compliance analysis was used to characterise how the suspension controls wheel orientation and wheel-centre movement through bounce, roll and steering inputs. Compliance behaviour was used to understand how the wheel assembly deflects under lateral and longitudinal loads due to stiffness in bushes, links, tyres and structural components.

The work connected suspension-level outputs to full-vehicle handling metrics such as understeer gradient, roll gradient, cornering compliance and maximum steady lateral acceleration. This made the project more than a plotting task: it was about using simulation outputs to understand vehicle behaviour.

02 — Technical context

Chassis Analysis and Simulations

The internship sat within chassis simulation work for passenger vehicles, where digital models are used to support design validation, reduce avoidable physical testing iterations and compare vehicle configurations before committing to hardware changes.

Why MBD was used

Multi-body dynamics is suited to suspension and steering systems because the vehicle is made up of linked bodies, joints, bushings, springs, dampers and tyre forces. It allows engineers to study how component-level motion affects full-vehicle behaviour.

Full-vehicle model structure

The full-vehicle model was structured around front suspension, rear suspension, steering system, vehicle body, load ballast system and tyre model subsystems.

Engineering value

03 — Kinematics and compliance

K&C Analysis Explained

Kinematics describes the geometry-driven motion of the wheels as the suspension moves through its design range. In this project, that included vertical travel, roll behaviour and steering response.

Compliance describes how much the suspension and wheel assembly deflect under load. This is important because real vehicle behaviour is not defined by rigid geometry alone; bushings, links, tyres and structural components all deform under lateral and longitudinal loads.

In practical chassis development, K&C acts like a suspension fingerprint. It helps engineers understand toe, camber, caster, bump steer, roll steer, compliance steer, wheel-centre movement and roll-centre characteristics before interpreting the vehicle-level handling response.

05 — Steady-state handling

Constant Radius Cornering and Handling Response

Steady-state handling describes how the vehicle responds to steering input when it is cornering in a controlled and repeatable condition. The method used in the report was Constant Radius Cornering, where the vehicle follows a circular path while speed is gradually increased.

As speed increases, lateral acceleration builds and the steering input required to maintain the same radius changes. This relationship is captured through the understeer gradient, which helps describe whether the vehicle tends towards understeer, neutral steer or oversteer.

The handling study also considered cornering compliance, roll gradient and maximum steady lateral acceleration. These metrics connect suspension behaviour to the driver-facing response of the vehicle.

06 — Correlation

CAE Model Versus Test Correlation

The final stage was CAE-model-versus-test interpretation. This matters because a simulation model is useful only when it reproduces the key physical trends with enough confidence for engineering decision-making.

The report presents similarity results across suspension kinematics, steering kinematics, suspension compliance and steady-state handling. The compared parameters include wheel rate, bump steer, camber change, caster change, roll stiffness, roll-centre height, steering angles, compliance steer, understeer gradient, cornering compliance, maximum steady lateral acceleration and roll gradient.

For the public portfolio, this should be shown as a recreated correlation-summary visual rather than raw internal tables. The message is that the model was evaluated for correlation quality, not that confidential test data is being published.
